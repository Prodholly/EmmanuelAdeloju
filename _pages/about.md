I am a third-year PhD student in Learning Literacies and Technology at Arizona State University, co-advised by [Dr. Michelle Jordan](https://search.asu.edu/profile/1623910) and [Dr. Punya Mishra](https://punyamishra.com/). My research focuses on Human-AI Interaction and Data Science Education, specifically, how we can use Large Language Models to support data sensemaking in science education.

My dissertation explores [how educators develop pedagogical and epistemic knowledge of LLMs as tools for data sensemaking]({{ '/research/' | relative_url }}), and how they scaffold students’ use of LLMs to construct evidence-based explanations from scientific data. I'm particularly interested in the intersection of AI capabilities and human learning. My research also examines how generative AI systems produce measurable socio-emotional risks in K-12 contexts, including parasocial attachment, anthropomorphization, and engineered manipulation, with direct implications for how AI design choices influence human psychology and behavior at scale. I use policy analysis and empirical methods to surface gaps between how AI systems are intended to behave and how they actually shape student agency, trust, and relational development. This work sits at the intersection of human-AI interaction, alignment, and model welfare.

What distinguishes me as a scholar is my interdisciplinary background in the sciences. Prior to my doctoral work, I earned an MS in Cell Biology and Genetics, where I conducted forensic hematology research, and a BS in Biochemistry, where I studied the renal toxicity of heavy metals in a local community. I also completed a Postgraduate Diploma in Chemistry Education, grounding my research in both scientific inquiry and pedagogical practice. This trajectory, from the lab bench to AI research, gives me deep insight into how scientists actually work with data and what authentic inquiry looks like.
