Guiding question 01 · Cognition

How do humans and AI construct knowledge from scientific multimodal data?

I study the cognitive processes through which people generate scientific knowledge from charts, tables, text and images, and I aim to build computational models that reproduce them. In parallel, I examine how AI systems represent, organize, retrieve and transform the same information. Comparing the two asks whether AI forms anything like a schema, and where its processes depart from ours.

Research questions

  1. Schema formation How do people form and revise schemas while making sense of data: noticing what doesn't fit, assimilating it, and restructuring when assimilation fails?
  2. Multimodal representation How do people coordinate charts, tables, text and images into one coherent representation of a phenomenon?
  3. Machine cognition Do large language models form schema-like structures? How do they represent, retrieve and transform information during a sensemaking task?
  4. Computational modeling Can a computational model of data sensemaking reproduce the processes and outcomes of human sensemaking, and so help explain them?

Where this starts

Preprint · EdArXiv · 2026

Data Sensemaking: A Theoretical Framework for Scientific Explanation and Knowledge Generation

Emmanuel Adeloju

The framework defines what a model of human sensemaking has to account for. It identifies four cognitive constructs that shape the process (schemas, anchors, frames and hypotheses), and specifies five dimensions, five developmental levels and four instructional principles for science classrooms.
