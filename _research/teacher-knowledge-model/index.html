Study design

The study is a comparative case study of two experienced in-service high school science teachers, each treated as a single case. The professional development program runs in three 4-hour phases (12 hours in total), and each phase makes a different part of the teachers' knowledge visible:

TAL · Teacher-as-Learner TAD · Teacher-as-Designer TAI · Teacher-as-Implementer
Teacher role Learner Designer Implementer
TPACK Initial technological, data-science, content, and pedagogical knowledge through experiencing AI-supported data science Transform emerging knowledge into instructional designs responsive to students, curriculum, context, and AI affordances/limitations Enact, evaluate, and adapt those learning and designs under authentic classroom conditions
Reflective practice Reflecting on emerging experiences and for design Reflecting on design decisions and for implementation Reflecting in, on, and for classroom practice
What this phase makes possible Gives teachers knowledge and experiences to draw upon when designing Makes teachers' pedagogical reasoning and intended decisions visible before classroom implementation Reveals how knowledge and design hold up, change, or break down in practice

Phase-oriented design: roles, knowledge, reflection, and analytic purpose (Table 2 of the paper).

Data include session recordings, classroom observations, reflection interviews, and teacher artifacts, analyzed within each teacher's trajectory and then across the two cases.
