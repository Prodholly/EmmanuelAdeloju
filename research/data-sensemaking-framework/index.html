Explaining with Data: A Framework for Data Sensemaking

Emmanuel Adeloju

In Preparation
Paper

Manuscript in progress. The full text will be posted when it's ready.

Abstract

Sensemaking has been theorized across cognitive science, information science, visual analytics, organizational studies, statistics and data science education, and science education, yet the constitutive role of data in explanation-building remains undertheorized. Data science education has examined how learners construct and represent data, while science education has theorized sensemaking and largely accepted data as a given input. I propose a theoretical framework for Data Sensemaking that occupies the intersection, defining it as the recursive process of constructing explanations of scientific phenomena that are grounded in data and governed by logic, with the goal of generating new knowledge. The framework distinguishes four routinely conflated cognitive constructs: schemas, anchors, frames, and hypotheses. It advances a three-level account of the epistemological status of data, as representation, as schematic artifact, and as epistemic object requiring evaluation, from which it derives data epistemic competence as a pedagogical target. The framework specifies five dimensions of data sensemaking across five developmental levels with observable indicators, and translates these into four instructional principles.

Overview

Students are often handed a finished dataset and asked what it shows, without ever asking where it came from or what it leaves out. This paper argues that making sense of data means both building an explanation and questioning the data behind it, and lays out what that looks like as learners grow from novice to expert.

Contributions

  • A definition: data sensemaking as the recursive, logic-governed construction of data-grounded explanations that generate new knowledge.
  • A cognitive account that separates schemas, anchors, frames, and hypotheses.
  • An epistemology of data: data as representation, as an artifact carrying its producers’ choices, and as evidence to be evaluated.
  • A developmental framework: five dimensions across five levels, from Absent to Expert, with observable indicators.

Instructional principles

  • Teach data provenance before and alongside analysis.
  • Introduce anomalies deliberately to disrupt premature closure.
  • Make evaluating explanations against multiple criteria routine.
  • Design for productive persistence rather than exhorting it.
