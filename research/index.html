TAL · Phase 1
Teacher-as-Learner
Direct User
Exploratory Interaction
4 hrs · 2 sessions
Interaction Modality
Teacher ↔ LLM (Direct)
Prompt-response loop. Teacher engages ChatGPT/LLM for data sensemakingconstruction of explanations from data, visualization, and interpretation tasks. Primarily conversational modality.
conversational AI
HAI Concept: Exploratory Use — user builds mental models through unstructured discovery within scaffolded task boundaries.
Knowledge Building Activity
Collaborative question framing → LLM refinement → Dataset identification. Teacher learns to treat LLM as epistemic partner.
epistemic scaffolding
HAI Concept: Cognitive Offloading — strategic delegation of cognitive tasks to AI while maintaining user agency over framing.
UX Events & HAI Constructs
Trust Calibration Moments
Surprise, confusion, and recalibration events when LLM outputs are unexpected, incomplete, or inaccurate.
reflection-in-action
RQ1 Touchpoint: Moments where teachers engage in reflection-in-action — updating mental models of AI capability in real time. Core data source for documenting reflective practice during TAL.
Affordance Recognition
Teacher identifies what LLM can/cannot do for data sensemaking. Develops TPACK-informed AI content knowledge (TK + CK).
TPACK formation
RQ2 Touchpoint: Teacher develops TPACK — shifts in technological knowledge (TK), content knowledge (CK), and their integration. Baseline perceptions of AI capability begin to be revised here.
↩ Recursive boundary — TAL insights actively reshape TAD design decisions. Teachers may re-enter TAL during TAD to test prompts.
TAD · Phase 2
Teacher-as-Designer
Co-Configuration
Human–AI–Human Triad
4 hrs · 2 sessions
Interaction Modality
Teacher + Researcher + LLM
Three-actor interaction triad. First time LLM shifts from tool to collaborator. Teacher + researcher co-prompt, evaluate, and refine AI outputs.
human-AI-human triad
HAI Concept: Collaborative Human-AI Teaming — distributed cognition across human and AI agents with negotiated agency and accountability.
Prompt Engineering as Design
Structured prompt engineering framework used to generate instructional materials. Teacher learns to configure AI outputs for pedagogical goals.
system shaping
HAI Concept: Appropriation — user adapts a general AI tool to fit specific professional and contextual needs.
UX Events & HAI Constructs
Participatory Design Moments
Teacher transitions from passive user to active system shaper. Evaluates AI lesson drafts for disciplinary and pedagogical alignment.
co-design agency
RQ3 Touchpoint: Co-design moments are key experiential elements — the specific decisions, negotiations, and surprises during TAD that most influence teachers' knowledge development and pedagogical choices.
Critical AI Evaluation
Teacher assesses LLM-generated instructional content for accuracy, relevance, and pedagogical fit. TAL-informed skepticism applied.
reflection-on-action
HAI Concept: Algorithmic Accountability — user critically interrogates AI outputs rather than accepting them uncritically.
↩ Recursive boundary — Co-designed artifacts carry embedded teacher knowledge into TAI. Implementation feedback may trigger TAD revision.

Teacher-as-Implementer
Interaction Broker
Hub-and-Spoke Model
Classroom Enactment
Interaction Modality
Teacher as Proxy Interactor
Teacher no longer primary AI user — now monitors and mediates 25-30 simultaneous student-AI interaction streams in real time.
interaction brokering
HAI Concept: Mediated Interaction — teacher operates as an intermediary layer between students and AI, shaping the quality and direction of student-AI interactions.
Student ↔ LLM Dyads (×N)
Multiple parallel student-AI interaction pairs. Students engage LLM for data sensemakingconstruction of explanations from data guided by teacher-designed scaffolds.
distributed HAI
HAI Concept: Distributed Human-AI Interaction — simultaneous, parallel human-AI dyads operating within a teacher-mediated environment.
UX Events & HAI Constructs
Real-Time Adaptive Facilitation
Teacher observes student-AI interactions, intervenes when prompts fail, recalibrates scaffolds mid-lesson. Reflection-in-action at scale.
reflection-in-action
RQ1 Touchpoint: Prime site for reflection-in-action at scale — teacher must simultaneously monitor AI interactions across 25-30 student dyads and adapt facilitation strategies in real time.
Post-Enactment Reflection
Reflection-on-action and reflection-for-action. Teacher evaluates student outcomes, AI behavior, and plans next iteration of design.
reflection-for-action
RQ1 + RQ3 Touchpoint: Reflection-on-action and reflection-for-action close the learning cycle. Post-enactment interviews capture which TAI experiences were most influential in shaping future pedagogical decisions.
Core HAI Architecture · Dual Interaction Loop

Two Nested Interaction Loops

The outer loop captures the Teacher–AI direct interaction across TAL and TAD phases — the teacher builds mental models, calibrates trust, and develops TPACK through iterative engagement with LLM-based tools.

The inner loop captures the Teacher–Student–AI facilitation loop during TAI — where the teacher's role shifts from direct user to interaction broker, orchestrating multiple simultaneous student-AI dyads.

HAI researchers would describe this nested structure as a multi-level human-AI teaming system with distinct interaction modalities at each layer.

cognitive offloading trust calibration appropriation supervisory control mental model formation algorithmic experience
Expected Outcomes · Aligned to Research Questions
RQ1
Reflective Practice Across Phases
Documents how teachers engage in reflection-in-action (adapting in real time during TAI), reflection-on-action (evaluating after TAL/TAD/TAI sessions), and reflection-for-action (planning forward iterations) — revealing the texture and function of reflective practice as teachers move through learning, designing, and implementing AI tools for data sensemakingconstruction of explanations from data.
Process · Schön's Reflective Practice
RQ2
Knowledge & Perception Shifts
Captures measurable shifts in teachers' perceptions of AI, subject matter knowledge of data science, and pedagogical content knowledge for AI-mediated instruction across TAL, TAD, and TAI. Quantified and characterized through mixed-methods instruments including TPACK surveys, interviews, and artifact analysis.
Substance · TPACK · TK+CK+PCK integration
RQ3
Influential Experiential Elements
Identifies which specific activities, interactions, and moments across the three phases were most influential in shaping teachers' reflective practice, knowledge development, and pedagogical decisions — providing design principles for future AI-integrated professional learning experiences in data science education.
